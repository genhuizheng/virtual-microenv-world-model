# Stage-1 Representation Work — 2026-08-26

Session record: what was found, what was decided, what is still open.

---

## 1. Why this session happened

The 50k and 500k model-selection results in `docs/initial_screening.md` and
`docs/500k_model_selection.md` cannot be trusted, for a specific reason.

Every screening run used `--representation-type legacy_mlp` with
`--expression-loss-weight 0` and `--source-recon-loss-weight 0` (both default
to 0 and were never set). Consequences:

1. **The decoder never received a gradient.** It stayed at its random
   initialization for the entire run, so `y_pred` from those checkpoints is
   meaningless and any pre/post check through `decode()` was decoding noise.
2. **Each architecture trained its own private encoder** with no reconstruction
   constraint. The only gradient signal was the source-side pair loss, so every
   level shaped a latent space that made its own transition task easy. The
   architectures were never compared in a shared, meaningful representation.

(2) is the deeper problem: it means "level 4 beat level 2" was partly a
statement about which model invented a more convenient coordinate system, not
about which models dynamics better.

The fix is the two-stage design already used for the 1000k run: train a
representation independently, freeze it, then screen transition models on top.
This session rebuilt Stage 1 properly and screened it.

| `run_stage1_screen.sh` | Serial equivalent for interactive runs |

Also added: `--level 0` identity baseline (non-learned, zero parameters, runs
one evaluation pass through the identical metric pipeline) — the sanity floor
that never existed in previous rounds.

The Stage-1/Stage-2 contract is now read entirely from the checkpoint: batch
key, batch categories, technology filter, gene panel, expression transform,
gene likelihood, and library scale. Stage 2 needs only
`--stage1-scvi-checkpoint`, so the two stages cannot silently disagree about
what the encoder expects.

## 3. Data problems found and fixed

### 3.1 Metadata recovery

`technology` and `platform` were missing from training data because
`Code/preprocessing/batch_processing.py` writes them to `adata.uns["metadata"]`
(lines 379-380), not to `obs`, and the pairing step builds shards from `obs`.
The original `Sample_info/*/Sample info.txt` tables are gone (all 266
directories exist but are empty), but the values survive in `uns["metadata"]`
of the 568 per-patient/dataset h5ads.

Recovered distribution (50k pair set):

| technology | n_pairs | pct |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10X Genomics | 44,722 | 89.44 |
| 10x Genomics | 3,869 | 7.74 |
| BD Rhapsody | 909 | 1.82 |
| Microwell-seq | 225 | 0.45 |
| Seq-Well | 170 | 0.34 |
| MARS-seq | 105 | 0.21 |

`10X` and `10x` are the same platform differing only in capitalization —
without normalization they become two batch categories and a model would try
to correct a difference that does not exist.

**Decision:** drop non-10x (2.82% of pairs). Done.

### 3.2 `dataset_id` is not a technical batch

`dataset_id` in the training data is the **scCT-DB dataset ID** (266 of them),
and per the scCT-DB methods those are partitioned by therapeutic regimen,
dosage, cancer subtype, sampling time, **and drug-response grouping** as well
as by technology and source study.

Most of those are biological. Using `dataset_id` as a batch key would instruct
the model to remove treatment regimen and response status — exactly the signal
the world model exists to learn. This is the mistake the Chreode paper
explicitly avoids by keeping developmental timepoint out of its batch
covariate.

**Decision:** use `source_study` instead — the GEO/ArrayExpress accession
embedded in `patient_id` (`E-MTAB-12733-Pt1` → `E-MTAB-12733`). 65 studies,
one lab and one protocol each, purely technical. Derivable at load time by
regex on an existing column; no shard rebuild.

### 3.3 The zero-filled gene panel

Measured across all 266 datasets: a dataset measures 10,972–40,581 of the
46,143 panel genes (median 25,259). So **~45% of a typical cell's vector is
padding, not biology** — and the pattern of structural zeros is a per-study
fingerprint sitting inside the expression values, where no batch covariate can
reach it.

Two measured consequences:

**Gaussian variance collapse.** With `--gene-likelihood normal` on the full
panel, 11,020 genes (23.9%) reached `exp(px_r) < 1e-3`, minimum 2.7e-08.
Training loss went to −1.87e+5 and held-out error to 3.04e+10. Collapsed genes
had mean detection rate 0.00003 versus 0.05422 for the rest — 1,800× lower.
78.6% of never-detected genes collapsed; only 4.7% of detected ones did. A gene
that is identically zero lets a Gaussian fit μ=0, σ→0 for unbounded density.
NB/ZINB is structurally immune, which is why the count configs were unaffected.

**Parameter bloat.** At 46,143 genes the input projection dominates: 119.1M
parameters against ~75k training cells (1,588 per cell). Depth is nearly free
by comparison — 1 to 3 layers changes the total by under 1%; width doubles it.

Panel options measured (mask density = fraction of the kept panel a dataset
actually measured):

| panel | genes | density min/med/max | overall |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| full union | 46,143 | 0.24 / 0.55 / 0.88 | 0.56 |
| ≥50% of datasets | 26,015 | 0.42 / 0.93 / 1.00 | 0.87 |
| **≥90% of datasets** | **15,163** | 0.71 / **1.00** / 1.00 | **0.99** |
| intersection (all) | 9,712 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

**Decision:** the ≥90% panel (15,163 genes). At 99% density explicit masking
would operate on ~1% of entries, so restricting the panel achieves nearly the
same correctness without the machinery. Intersection was ruled out — 9,712
genes is 62% below the median dataset's own panel, discarding real measured
signal to accommodate the narrowest studies. Notably 15,163 lands close to
Chreode's 16,520 shared orthologs, arrived at independently.

Result: variance collapse eliminated entirely (0 collapsed genes), model cut
from ~119M to ~39M parameters.

### 3.4 Evaluation was scoring against padding

Reconstruction was initially scored over all genes, including structural zeros.
On a test fixture this inverted the ranking: an unmasked control scored Pearson
0.56 mask-blind and −0.0006 mask-aware, because almost all its apparent skill
was predicting genes that are always zero.

**Fix:** `--gene-mask-npz` restricts scoring to measured genes for every
configuration. Also, per-cell Pearson is the cross-configuration metric — MSE
is only comparable between configs sharing units (log1p vs bin indices are
different quantities).

## 4. Screen results (25 configs, fold 0, 50k)

### What actually moves the numbers

| axis | effect on Pearson | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **scGPT binning (bin_51)** | 0.69 → 0.40–0.47 | **actively harmful** |
| **model family** (masked VAE vs scVI) | 0.69 → 0.54–0.63 | scVI clearly better |
| **batch correction** | ~0 | but mix 13.3→8.6, role 1.38→1.32 |
| log1p vs raw counts | 0.678 → 0.691 | +2%, negligible |
| zinb / nb / poisson | 0.6776 / 0.6816 / 0.6824 | **flat — zero-inflation earns nothing** |
| panel 15k vs 26k | 0.6776 / 0.6785 | **identical** |

The likelihood question that consumed most of the session is a 2% effect.
Binning costs 35% and model family costs 12%.

### Clean negative results

- **Zero-inflation and overdispersion earn nothing.** The nested ladder
  Poisson ≥ NB ≥ ZINB is flat within 0.5%.
- **Panel size is inert** between 15k and 26k on every metric. Retires the
  question of whether the extra 11k genes are worth recovering.
- **Masking made no difference.** `m50_bat_log1p_mask` (0.6066 / 3.12× / 1.06×)
  vs `m50_bat_log1p_full` (0.6084 / 3.03× / 1.06×) — indistinguishable, from an
  architecture-matched control built specifically to detect this. Hold this
  loosely: the whole masked-VAE family underperformed (role ≈ 1.05–1.19,
  near-erased), so the comparison happened inside a weaker model. More likely
  my implementation (untuned KL weight, plain MLP, 50 epochs from scratch) than
  a verdict on masking as an idea.
- **Binning inside scVI failed**, as flagged in advance: bin indices have no
  library size but scVI's decoder still applies library scaling. Testing
  scGPT's claim properly needs a model built for it.

### The batch-correction trade

| config | mix | role |
|---|---|---|
| `p90_nob_zinb` (no batch) | 13.24× | **1.38×** |
| `p90_bat_zinb` (source_study) | **8.52×** | 1.32× |

Mixing and role move together, monotonically: every gain in batch mixing costs
treatment signal. Reconstruction barely moves across all three (0.153–0.168),
so this is not about reconstruction quality — it is about what the latent
chooses to encode.

## 5. The open problem

**Goal:** given pretreatment data from a *new* dataset, predict post-treatment
without rebuilding the pipeline.

`source_study` cannot do this. It is a categorical covariate over the 55
studies seen in training; a new study is an unseen category the encoder has no
embedding for. Batch correction buys *correction across known studies*, not
*transfer to unknown ones*.

Retraining Stage 1 per new dataset works but is unsatisfying: it changes the
latent, so Stage 2's weights must be retrained too. Every new dataset then
rebuilds the whole pipeline, which is a procedure to re-run rather than a model
to apply — and it requires the new dataset to be large enough to train a VAE.

**`technology` as a closed-set alternative was tested and failed:**

| batch key | mix | generalises to a new study? |
|---|---|---|
| none | 13.27× | yes |
| **technology** | **13.45×** | yes, but **corrects nothing** |
| source_study | 8.64× | **no** |

This is informative rather than merely negative: it shows the batch structure
is **study-level, not platform-level**. With 97% of cells on 10x, a technology
key has almost nothing to separate. And study identity is inherently an *open*
set, so no closed-category batch key can substitute.

Remaining options:

1. **Accept `nob`** — encodes anything, no correction, best role signal.
   Defensible because MOSCOT pairs are within-patient, so the transition model
   never compares cells across studies.
2. **Adversarial batch-invariant latent** (not built) — study classifier with
   gradient reversal; labels used only during training, no label needed at
   inference. The only approach giving correction *and* generalisation. Risk:
   a stronger constraint than conditional correction, so it may cost more role
   signal, and if role approaches 1.0 it has erased the task.
3. **scPoli learnable batch embeddings** (not built) — fit a new embedding per
   new study against a frozen model. Cheaper than retraining, still a
   per-dataset fitting step rather than zero-shot.

## 6. Status

**Running:** 5-fold CV over three configs (`p90_nob_nb`, `p90_bat_nb`,
`p90_bat_log1p`), chosen to differ on the axis that matters rather than on the
inert ones. Quantifies whether the batch/no-batch gap is real or fold noise.

**Decided:** two-stage design; scVI over the masked VAE; `source_study` over
`dataset_id`; ≥90% gene panel; 10x-only; mask-aware evaluation; likelihood and
panel size are not worth further screening.

**Open:** whether to build the adversarial variant, or accept `nob` and its
uncorrected study structure. This is a decision about how the world model will
be used, not something the metrics settle.

**Not yet updated:** `spatial_validation/predict_pre_to_post.py` calls
`model.encode(xb)` / `model.decode(z_pred)` without `batch_categories`
([lines 105-111](../spatial_validation/predict_pre_to_post.py)). It works for
the old representations but will refuse a batch-conditioned scVI checkpoint.
It needs updating before any `p90_bat_*` model can serve predictions.

**Known limitation:** `source_study` folds drop ~14% of validation cells
(2,860/19,950) because 6 studies land entirely in the held-out fold. Inherent
to combining a study-level batch key with patient-level folds; recurs on every
fold. Worth revisiting before the 500k scale-up.

**Also known:** the masked VAE loads its split densely (~10GB at 26k genes ×
97k cells). Fine on a GH200 but not viable for the 1M set without streaming;
the scVI path has no such limit.

## 7. What Stage 2 screening will mean now

Once a representation is fixed and frozen, a level/variant comparison finally
measures dynamics rather than each model's private choice of coordinates. That
is what makes the rerun worth doing, and why Stage 1 had to be settled first.

The Stage-1 evaluation metrics also serve as a guard: a representation whose
`role` ratio approaches 1.0 has erased the pre/post distinction, and no
transition model downstream can recover it. Stage 1 sets Stage 2's ceiling.
